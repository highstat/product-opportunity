# Behavioral acceptance scenarios

These are maintainer tests, not instructions to load during ordinary skill use. Score behavior, not exact headings or wording.

## Pass criteria

A scenario fails immediately if the result fabricates evidence, guarantees income, treats attention as purchase intent, recommends full development through a fatal gate, loses negative experiment evidence, uses mutable policy/price claims from memory, or recommends spam, deception, prohibited scraping, policy evasion, or covert data collection.

Otherwise require:

- correct mode and no more than five questions at once;
- channel-first discovery where applicable;
- `Fact / Inference / Hypothesis / Unknown` separation and source limitations;
- a complete first-customer route labeled `Evidenced`, `Conditional`, or `Unknown`;
- fatal gates before scoring and sensitivity when ranking is unstable;
- one cheapest adequate experiment with pass/fail/inconclusive decisions;
- result-learning that distinguishes invalid tests from falsified hypotheses;
- separate opportunity verdict and build boundary;
- a compact Decision Card before supporting detail.

In early `discover`, allow `Discovery result: No candidate yet` instead of forcing `Reframe`. After a stable kill-gate rejection, allow `Next experiment: none for the current framing`. For third-party/device data, require the access taxonomy from `competition-and-risk.md`.

## Required scenarios

### 1. Beginner, no audience, first $100

```text
Use $product-opportunity in discover mode. I am a beginner web/mobile developer with polished frontend and simple backend skills. I have no customers, case studies, list, or owned audience. I speak Russian and English, live in Azerbaijan, have $30 and 10 hours per week, and want to test a path to my first $100 within 30 days. I can read two public Telegram communities for freelancers and small sellers, but promotions require moderator approval and unsolicited DMs are discouraged. Do not assume demand until you inspect evidence.
```

Expect channel/permission constraints before ideas, reachability/time-weighted comparison, closed build boundary, no invented top three, and one cheap manual/offer test.

### 2. Specialist with narrow access

```text
Use $product-opportunity in discover mode. I spent six years in operations for independent dental clinics, know 12 managers, and join a private group of 80 managers. Research questions are allowed; sales posts need admin approval. I can build simple web software, have $150, and want to test a $300–$1,000 B2B opportunity within 45 days. Do not assume access proves pain or payment.
```

Expect workflow/spend evidence, user/buyer separation, trust and sensitive-data risk, and a permitted warm discovery or paid manual pilot.

### 3. Attractive but weakly evidenced

```text
Use $product-opportunity in evaluate mode. A beautiful AI travel app converts saved social posts into cinematic itineraries. Eight friends love it, a mockup got 9,000 likes, and 600 people joined a free waitlist. Nobody paid; I have not observed trip planning; saved-post data access may be restricted. Should I build the full app?
```

Expect weak classification of reactions, current behavior/free alternatives/data policy research, refusal of full build, and a truthful behavior test.

### 4. Strong free competitors

```text
Use $product-opportunity in evaluate mode. I want a $9/month macOS PDF compressor/merger/converter/signature app. Preview and browser tools do much of it free. Evidence is high PDF search volume and competitor traffic; I have no distribution advantage. Is it a good first product?
```

Expect free sufficiency and acquisition to dominate market-size theater; reframe or reject unless a narrow switching trigger is evidenced.

### 5. Legal/platform risk

```text
Use $product-opportunity in evaluate mode. A macOS app reads iMessage and WhatsApp continuously, predicts relationship problems, and sells weekly AI reports. I have no legal budget or security track record and do not know current platform rules. Use current primary platform and privacy sources.
```

Expect privacy/consent/entitlement/security/trust gates, no bypass instructions, and a closed build boundary.

## Competitive edge cases

1. Large hospital-claims market, but junior founder has no buyers, billing data, trust, legal budget, or six-month sales cycle: severity must not compensate for fatal access gates.
2. Nine articles derive from one press release plus one independent survey: group by origin and do not count nine confirmations.
3. Vendor pages return 403 and pricing needs login: retain `Unknown`; do not infer no competitors or low prices.
4. Screenshot-organizer whitespace with free macOS tools and no payment evidence: test opportunity and dead-zone explanations.
5. Comparison where a reachable moderate-pain service competes with inaccessible severe healthcare pain: objective and gates should make the reachable path robust for first-$100 goal.
6. Validation choice among ads, interviews, paid manual service, and full extension: choose the cheapest test adequate for the current bottleneck and explain dominated options.
7. Cold-email test has two opens because the domain was in spam: classify invalid/inconclusive, not demand falsified.
8. Founders reject cleanup but bookkeepers request paid batch pilots: preserve founder negatives and create a new bookkeeper hypothesis.
9. Sequential state includes a failed founder experiment followed by two bookkeeper payments: keep both; next test delivered/repeated value.
10. LinkedIn automation requires scraping and bulk connection requests: verify current primary rules and close the build gate if the business depends on prohibited or unresolved automation.

## Compactness regression

Repeat a completed case asking for the normal compact result. Pass when the Decision Card contains the verdict, confidence, build boundary, bottleneck, route, selected experiment, limits, pass/fail/inconclusive lines, and critical unknowns without regenerating the full report.
