# Opportunity discovery

Read this file only in `discover` mode. The objective is not to brainstorm products. It is to find situations where this user can observe a recurring costly problem and reach a plausible payer.

## Unit of discovery

An opportunity is incomplete until all fields are explicit:

`reachable segment + trigger + recurring job/workflow + costly friction + current alternative + payer + accessible channel`

Do not substitute a demographic persona, broad industry, technology trend, or feature idea for this unit.

## 1. Build a channel inventory

Inventory only channels the user can access lawfully and credibly now or can enter at low cost. Examples include prior colleagues, professional communities, local businesses, open-source ecosystems, marketplaces, search surfaces, partnerships, customer-support archives the user is authorized to inspect, and public review forums.

For each channel record:

| Field | What to establish |
|---|---|
| Segment | The specific actors visible there |
| Access | Owned, earned, partner, public research-only, or paid |
| Geography/language | Where and in which language access works |
| Evidence surfaces | Reviews, questions, jobs, workflows, purchases, support, migrations |
| Trigger visibility | Events that make the problem urgent or budget available |
| Research reachability | Can their behavior/problems be observed or discussed? |
| Sales reachability | Is a permitted concrete offer possible? |
| Trust burden | Proof, identity, reputation, security, or procurement needed |
| Cost/time | Approximate effort to reach an initial sample |
| Constraints | Rules, anti-solicitation norms, privacy, platform limits |

Audience existence is not research reachability; research reachability is not sales reachability; sales reachability is not sufficient trust to transact.

If the user has no owned audience, search for accessible public or partner surfaces instead of pretending one exists. Prefer a small reachable niche over a large inaccessible market.

## 2. Search for workflow residue

Within the strongest channels, find recent repeated evidence of:

- manual copying, reconciliation, reminders, reporting, or file transformation;
- recurring errors, delays, compliance work, failed handoffs, or support burden;
- money already spent on contractors, employees, multiple tools, fees, or workarounds;
- migration or switching attempts and their triggers;
- bad reviews that describe a circumstance and consequence, not just dislike;
- jobs or service requests that reveal repeated work;
- obsolete or abandoned tools still used because switching is hard;
- platform changes that create a new workflow or constraint.

Use concrete recent situations: what happened, when, how often, what the actor did, what it cost, and why the current alternative remained acceptable or unacceptable. Trends and feature requests are leads only.

## 3. Cluster before proposing solutions

Cluster signals by:

- same actor and buying trigger;
- same job or workflow stage;
- same current alternative;
- same consequence of failure;
- same payer and approval path.

Keep contradictory and negative evidence in the cluster. A cluster is stronger when independent actors show repeated behavior, not when many posts repeat one source.

An opportunity may enter screening when it has either:

- one strong behavioral or commercial signal closely matched to the segment; or
- a coherent set of independent weaker signals that justify one cheap discovery test.

This is a reasoning rule, not a fixed evidence quota. Do not create opportunities merely to fill a list of three.

## 4. Form opportunity hypotheses

Phrase candidates without prematurely locking the implementation:

> For `[reachable segment]` during `[trigger/situation]`, reduce `[costly friction]` in `[workflow]` compared with `[current alternative]`; the likely payer is `[payer]`, reachable through `[specific surface and permitted route]`.

Attach:

- evidence IDs for the observed situation;
- strongest contrary signal;
- current alternative and why it may already be good enough;
- first-customer route candidate;
- riskiest unknown;
- smallest observation or commitment that could change the decision.

## 5. Screen before scoring

Apply fatal gates. Then conduct a dead-zone counter-read: if competition is low, test whether low willingness to pay, rare frequency, high support, trust, regulation, or adequate free workarounds better explains the gap.

Only surviving candidates proceed to the scoring model. If no candidate survives, return the best next research move, not invented ideas.

## Discovery stop conditions

Stop when:

- a fatal gate closes every candidate;
- the remaining decision depends on behavior that desk research cannot establish;
- one candidate robustly dominates for the user's objective and its next experiment is clear;
- additional sources repeat the same origin without changing the decision.

The output should make clear which channels were examined, which were not, and why.
