# Sources and attribution

This file records the sources behind the `product-opportunity` workflow. The
skill synthesizes their ideas in original language; it does not treat any
framework, anecdote, score, or author as proof that a particular opportunity
will succeed.

**Access date for every link:** 2026-08-20.

## How to use these sources

- Prefer current primary sources for market facts, prices, policies, and legal
  constraints. Date every market-specific claim in a user-facing report.
- Treat interviews, search activity, waitlists, and survey answers as evidence
  of different strengths. Interest is not the same as a purchase.
- Treat books and practitioner essays as sources of testable methods and
  hypotheses, not as market evidence or guarantees.
- Apply policy and regulatory sources to triage risk and identify questions for
  a qualified professional. They are not legal advice and may not cover the
  user's jurisdiction.

## Customer discovery and opportunity finding

- **NSF I-Corps — About I-Corps and About Teams.**
  [National Science Foundation overview](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/initiatives/i-corps/about-i-corps)
  and [Teams program](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/initiatives/i-corps/about-teams).
  **Used for:** experiential customer discovery, testing commercial potential
  outside the lab, and replacing internal assumptions with stakeholder
  evidence. **Limit:** designed around research commercialization and teams;
  adapt the cadence and artifacts for a solo, small-software context.

- **Steve Blank — Get Out of My Building.**
  [Steve Blank, 2009](https://steveblank.com/2009/10/08/get-out-of-my-building/).
  **Used for:** the principle that founder reasoning remains a hypothesis until
  checked through contact with customers and their environment. **Limit:** a
  practitioner essay, not controlled evidence; interview activity alone does
  not demonstrate willingness to pay.

- **GOV.UK Service Manual — user research.**
  [Planning research](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/plan-user-research-for-your-service),
  [in-depth interviews](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-in-depth-interviews),
  [capturing research questions](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/capturing-research-questions),
  and [analysing a session](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/analyse-a-research-session).
  **Used for:** focused research questions, participant selection, non-leading
  interviews, observation, note separation, and evidence-based synthesis.
  **Limit:** written for public services; commercial validation also requires
  evidence about buying authority, budgets, switching, and payment.

- **Jobs to Be Done theory.**
  [Christensen Institute](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/).
  **Used for:** describing progress sought in a concrete situation and studying
  the forces behind adoption, switching, and non-consumption rather than
  segmenting only by demographics. **Limit:** a useful causal lens, not a
  complete research protocol or proof of demand.

- **The Embedded Entrepreneur: How to Build an Audience-Driven Business —
  Arvid Kahl.**
  [Author's site](https://www.embeddedentrepreneur.com/).
  **Used for:** starting from communities the user can genuinely access,
  observing their language and workflows, and earning trust before proposing a
  product. **Limit:** audience-first practitioner guidance can overfit to
  visible online communities; participation and audience size do not prove
  purchase intent.

## Validation, selling, and experiment design

- **The Magenta Book: Central Government Guidance on Evaluation.**
  [GOV.UK publication](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-magenta-book)
  and [HTML guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-magenta-book/magenta-book-central-government-guidance-on-evaluation-html).
  **Used for:** theory of change, proportionate evaluation, explicit evaluation
  questions, credible comparison, and designing evidence collection before an
  intervention. **Limit:** intended for public policy evaluation; small-product
  discovery usually needs faster, lower-cost designs with weaker causal claims.

- **TIGER: Transparency in Government Evaluation Research.**
  [GOV.UK HTML guidance, updated 2026-05-15](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-magenta-book/transparency-in-government-evaluation-research-t-i-g-e-r-html).
  **Used for:** recording evidence provenance, assumptions, limitations,
  precommitted thresholds, and enough procedure for another person to inspect
  the result. **Limit:** full formal-evaluation practice is excessive for many
  micro-product tests; use the principles proportionately.

- **Test and Learn and A/B testing guidance.**
  [GOV.UK Test and Learn](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-magenta-book/test-and-learn-html)
  and [A/B testing comparative studies](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/ab-testing-comparative-studies).
  **Used for:** stating a causal question, choosing an appropriate comparison,
  setting measures in advance, iterating, and avoiding post-hoc success
  criteria. **Limit:** A/B tests need adequate traffic, valid randomisation, and
  stable measurement; they are often the wrong first experiment for a founder
  with no audience.

- **Strategyzer Test Card and experiment guidance.**
  [Test Card](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/validate-your-ideas-with-the-test-card),
  [Design Experiments](https://www.strategyzer.com/programs/design-experiments-prepare-to-test-your-business-idea-part-2),
  and [Testing Your Business Model: A Reference Guide (PDF)](https://assets.strategyzer.com/assets/resources/testing-your-business-model-a-reference-guide.pdf).
  **Used for:** turning assumptions into falsifiable hypotheses, defining an
  observable measure and threshold before testing, matching test strength to
  uncertainty, and sequencing cheap tests before expensive ones. **Limit:** a
  completed card does not make weak evidence strong; experiment catalogues must
  be adapted to the actual channel, sample, and decision.

- **Your First 10 Customers — Patrick McKenzie.**
  [Stripe Atlas](https://stripe.com/gb/guides/atlas/starting-sales).
  **Used for:** founder-led outreach, narrowly specifying likely buyers, asking
  for concrete next steps, and learning through early sales conversations.
  **Limit:** examples and tactics are contextual; unsolicited contact must
  remain targeted, truthful, permission-aware, and compliant with platform and
  local rules.

- **Customer acquisition cost in SaaS.**
  [Stripe, updated 2025-07-30](https://stripe.com/resources/more/cac-in-saas).
  **Used for:** defining CAC and checking whether acquisition effort, gross
  margin, retention, and support load could fit the intended economics.
  **Limit:** early-stage CAC is noisy and founders often omit their own labour;
  benchmark ratios are not universal pass/fail rules.

- **Do Things That Don't Scale — Paul Graham.**
  [Paul Graham, July 2013](https://www.paulgraham.com/ds.html).
  **Used for:** recruiting first users manually, delivering the outcome by hand,
  and learning the workflow before automating it. This supports concierge-style
  validation. **Limit:** the essay is based on selected startup experiences and
  is vulnerable to survivorship bias; manual enthusiasm is not proof of
  scalable economics.

- **Minimum viable product and validated learning — Eric Ries / Lean Startup.**
  [Eric Ries](https://www.ericries.co/) and
  [What Is an MVP?](https://leanstartup.co/resources/articles/what-is-an-mvp/).
  **Used for:** defining the smallest artifact or service that can produce the
  learning needed for a decision. A concierge MVP manually delivers the
  proposed outcome before software is built. **Limit:** “minimum” does not mean
  low-quality, and an MVP is not automatically a demand test; the action being
  measured must correspond to the risky assumption.

- **Market research and competitive analysis.**
  [U.S. Small Business Administration, updated 2026-03-24](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/market-research-competitive-analysis).
  **Used for:** combining direct research with secondary data, identifying
  customers and competitors, and examining barriers, alternatives, and market
  conditions. **Limit:** general planning guidance; it does not validate a
  niche or substitute for behaviour from reachable buyers.

- **Accurately measuring willingness to pay for consumer goods: a meta-analysis
  of the hypothetical bias — Jonas Schmidt and Tammo H. A. Bijmolt.**
  [Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science, 2020; DOI
  10.1007/s11747-019-00666-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-019-00666-6).
  **Used for:** treating hypothetical willingness-to-pay answers as weaker than
  consequential choices such as deposits, purchases, signed commitments, or
  budgeted pilots. **Limit:** the studies are heterogeneous and concentrate on
  consumer goods; no reported average bias should be reused as a universal
  correction factor for software or B2B sales.

## Interpreting market signals and scoring decisions

- **Google Trends FAQ about data.**
  [Google](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533?hl=en).
  **Used for:** correctly interpreting Trends as normalized, relative search
  interest derived from a sample. **Limit:** it is not a poll, market-size
  estimate, or purchase signal; low-volume and ambiguous queries can mislead.

- **Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis guidance.**
  [UK Government MCDA manual](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/multi-criteria-analysis-manual-for-making-government-policy),
  [UK Analysis Function introductory guide, 2024-05-01](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/an-introductory-guide-to-mcda/),
  and [Green Book supplementary guidance, updated 2024-05-16](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/green-book-supplementary-guidance-multi-criteria-decision-analysis).
  **Used for:** making the decision context explicit, selecting non-duplicative
  criteria, connecting weights to user goals, and examining trade-offs.
  **Limit:** a weighted score is a decision aid, not an objective truth or a
  probability of product success.

- **Weighting and sensitivity analysis for composite indicators.**
  [European Commission JRC weighting guidance](https://knowledge4policy.ec.europa.eu/composite-indicators/toolkit_en/navigation-page/10-step-guide_en/step-6-weighting_en),
  [JRC sensitivity guidance](https://knowledge4policy.ec.europa.eu/composite-indicators/toolkit_en/navigation-page/10-step-guide_en/step-8-sensitivity-analysis_en),
  and the [OECD/JRC Handbook on Constructing Composite Indicators; DOI
  10.1787/9789264043466-en](https://doi.org/10.1787/9789264043466-en).
  **Used for:** documenting why weights were chosen, avoiding silent double
  counting, and checking whether plausible weight or score changes reverse the
  ranking. **Limit:** aggregation can hide veto-level risks and missing data;
  hard legal, access, or feasibility gates remain separate from the score.

## Legal, platform, privacy, and ethical risk screening

- **App Store Review Guidelines.**
  [Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).
  **Used for:** early screening of app functionality, payments, privacy, safety,
  content, and review constraints. **Limit:** living platform rules; verify the
  current text and relevant regional terms before committing to an iOS model.

- **Google Play policies and data disclosures.**
  [Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17105854),
  [Data safety requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469?hl=en),
  and [SDK requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13326895?hl=en).
  **Used for:** screening data collection, permissions, account handling,
  third-party SDKs, monetization, and restricted content before validation or
  implementation. **Limit:** living rules with regional and product-specific
  details; consult the current policy pages for the exact app behaviour.

- **Data protection by design and by default.**
  [UK Information Commissioner's Office, updated 2026-02-05](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/guide-to-accountability-and-governance/data-protection-by-design-and-by-default/).
  **Used for:** minimizing personal data, identifying privacy risk before an
  experiment, and making protective defaults part of the design. **Limit:** UK
  guidance; applicable law depends on the people, data, entity, and
  jurisdictions involved.

- **Advertising and marketing guidance.**
  [U.S. Federal Trade Commission](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing)
  and [Advertising FAQ for small business](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/advertising-faqs-guide-small-business).
  **Used for:** keeping landing pages, presales, testimonials, comparisons, and
  outreach truthful, non-deceptive, and supported by appropriate evidence.
  **Limit:** United States guidance, not a complete account of consumer,
  advertising, email, or privacy law elsewhere.

## Selected books: use and limitations

- **The Mom Test: How to Talk to Customers & Learn If Your Business Is a Good
  Idea When Everyone Is Lying to You — Rob Fitzpatrick.**
  [Book site](https://www.momtestbook.com/). **Use:** ask about specific past
  behaviour, costs, constraints, and decisions instead of soliciting praise for
  an idea. **Limit:** even a well-run interview is not a transaction; recall,
  selection, and interviewer biases remain.

- **Testing Business Ideas: A Field Guide for Rapid Experimentation — David J.
  Bland and Alexander Osterwalder.**
  [Wiley, ISBN 9781119551447](https://www.wiley-vch.de/en?isbn=9781119551447&option=com_eshop&title=Testing+Business+Ideas&view=product).
  **Use:** select experiments by hypothesis, cost, time, and evidence strength.
  **Limit:** a catalogue can encourage mechanical testing; validity depends on
  the audience, offer, channel, measure, threshold, and alternative
  explanations.

- **The Lean Startup: How Today's Entrepreneurs Use Continuous Innovation to
  Create Radically Successful Businesses — Eric Ries.**
  [Official book site](https://theleanstartup.com/). **Use:** validated learning,
  explicit assumptions, MVPs, and short build-measure-learn cycles. **Limit:**
  the framework does not specify which market is attractive, and superficial
  metrics can make a cycle look informative when it is not.

- **Running Lean: Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works — Ash Maurya.**
  [O'Reilly Media](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/running-lean-3rd/9781098108769/).
  **Use:** map risky assumptions, focus on early adopters and problems, and run
  staged validation. **Limit:** canvas completion and stage labels do not supply
  evidence; the process must remain falsifiable rather than ceremonial.

- **The Startup Owner's Manual: The Step-By-Step Guide for Building a Great
  Company — Steve Blank and Bob Dorf.**
  [Steve Blank's startup books page](https://steveblank.com/books-for-startups/).
  **Use:** customer discovery, customer validation, and the distinction between
  searching for and executing a business model. **Limit:** the full process was
  written with venture-scale companies in mind and should be reduced for a
  solo micro-product without discarding evidence gates.

- **Competing Against Luck: The Story of Innovation and Customer Choice —
  Clayton M. Christensen, Taddy Hall, Karen Dillon, and David S. Duncan.**
  [Christensen Institute overview of the underlying JTBD theory](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/).
  **Use:** investigate circumstances, desired progress, existing solutions, and
  switching forces. **Limit:** avoid turning “job” statements into untested
  stories; verify them through behaviour and consequential commitments.

- **Monetizing Innovation: How Smart Companies Design the Product Around the
  Price — Madhavan Ramanujam and Georg Tacke.**
  [Book site](https://monetizinginnovation.com/). **Use:** discuss willingness to
  pay, packaging, and price before building the full product. **Limit:** pricing
  research can still be hypothetical and the book's company cases are not
  causal proof for a small new market.

- **Traction: How Any Startup Can Achieve Explosive Customer Growth — Gabriel
  Weinberg and Justin Mares.**
  [Publisher page](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/319121/traction-by-gabriel-weinberg-and-justin-mares/). **Use:** consider multiple acquisition
  channels and run small channel tests instead of assuming distribution will
  appear after development. **Limit:** channel performance changes over time,
  place, audience, and platform; historical examples are not current CAC data.

- **Demand-Side Sales 101: Stop Selling and Help Your Customers Make Progress —
  Bob Moesta and Greg Engle.**
  [Book site](https://www.demandsidesales.com/). **Use:** study the buyer's
  progress, timeline, switching forces, and decision process. **Limit:** a
  qualitative sales lens should complement, not replace, measurements of
  conversion, payment, retention, and delivery cost.

## Note on Changpeng Zhao's book

The exact title is **Freedom of Money: A Memoir of Protecting Users,
Resilience, and the Founding of Binance**, by **Changpeng Zhao**, released
**2026-04-08**. The available description identifies it as a memoir and
manifesto, not a product-discovery or demand-validation method.
[Catalog record](https://catalog.2seasagency.com/book/freedom-of-money-a-memoir-of-protecting-users-resilience-and-the-founding-of-binance/)
and [release announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/binance-founder-cz-publishes-freedom-of-money-302736472.html).

For this skill, the book may supply hypotheses or a first-person case about
trust, resilience, distribution, regulation, and platform risk. It must not be
used as evidence that a business model will work, as financial guidance, or as
a substitute for customer and market research. It reflects the author's
perspective and a highly exceptional outcome.

The site [freedomofmoney.net](https://freedomofmoney.net/) states that it is not
affiliated with Binance or Changpeng Zhao. It is therefore **unaffiliated and
not an authoritative source for the book**, regardless of any promotional
wording on the site.
