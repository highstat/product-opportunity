# First-customer path

Use this in `discover`, `evaluate`, and `validate`. A channel label is not a customer-acquisition plan. Require a traceable, permitted route from a real surface to a concrete buyer action.

## Route card

Complete this sequence for each finalist:

`buyer + trigger → current surface → qualification → permitted access → trust bridge → narrow offer → concrete ask → counted behavior → refusal taxonomy → fallback`

Prefix every route `Evidenced`, `Conditional`, or `Unknown`. Moderator approval, API approval, marketing consent, a future partnership, or “could run ads” remains `Conditional` until obtained and checked.

| Field | Required evidence or decision |
|---|---|
| Buyer and trigger | Who can pay, and what event makes action timely? |
| Current surface | One opened, current URL or a user-owned channel; date checked |
| Activity fit | Evidence that the target segment and trigger actually appear there |
| Qualification | Observable rule for including a prospect; avoid guessed identity |
| Permitted access | Posting, reply, introduction, marketplace response, search intent, or partner route allowed by rules and norms |
| Trust bridge | Relevant proof, sample, guarantee boundary, reference, partner, or low-risk first step |
| Narrow offer | Outcome and scope small enough for an early buyer to evaluate |
| Concrete ask | A meeting, paid manual service, pilot, deposit, procurement step, or other observable commitment |
| Counted behavior | What action counts, with an exposure denominator where possible |
| Refusal taxonomy | No problem, no urgency, no trust, no budget, wrong buyer, current alternative sufficient, channel/message failure |
| Fallback route | A materially different permitted route, not more volume through the same failed mechanism |

## Four separate tests

Never collapse these into “reachable”:

1. **Audience existence:** the segment exists on the surface.
2. **Research reachability:** their workflow can be observed or discussed.
3. **Sales reachability:** a relevant offer can be made lawfully and within platform rules.
4. **Transaction trust:** the user can plausibly satisfy identity, security, proof, payment, support, and procurement expectations.

A failure at one layer is a specific unknown or gate. A public community may be excellent for research and prohibit promotion. A marketplace may permit offers but impose reputation barriers.

## Evidence requirements

- Open at least one current surface; record retrieval date and limitations.
- Prefer evidence of current activity and visible buying/problem triggers over member count.
- Check the surface's current rules when outreach or promotion is material.
- Record whether access is owned, earned, partner-mediated, public research-only, or paid.
- Estimate time and cash to reach an initial relevant sample; include the founder's time.
- Do not claim conversion rates without applicable evidence. Use the first experiment to measure them.

## Safe outreach boundary

Planning a route does not authorize contact. Do not recommend bulk unsolicited messages, prohibited scraping, purchased personal lists, impersonation, deceptive urgency, or evasion of moderation. Favor opted-in requests, relevant public replies where allowed, introductions, transparent posts, marketplaces, partners, and intent-bearing search surfaces.

## Decision rules

- If no credible route exists, close the build boundary and `Reframe` or `Reject`.
- If a route exists for research but not selling, validate the problem only and keep monetization unknown.
- If trust is the bottleneck, test the trust bridge or choose a lower-trust offer before testing product features.
- If the route is plausible but unmeasured, make reachability the decision bottleneck and run a small route microtest.
- If one route fails, use refusal and exposure data to distinguish audience, qualification, message, trust, and channel failure before killing the problem.
